Who holds up when the match is on the line

A model of pressure built from 201,132 balls of IPL and T20 International cricket, 2022 to 2026. Every ball is scored by how far it can move the result, and every player is judged against his own usual level.

The top 10 under pressure

    What counts as pressure

    Pressure here is leverage: the expected size of the swing in win probability that a single ball can cause. A win-probability model reads the match state before every ball, an outcome model gives the odds of a dot, a single, a boundary, a wicket or an extra, and leverage is the probability-weighted swing across those outcomes.

    A ball counts as a pressure ball when its leverage is in the top quarter for its phase of the innings, so openers, middle-order batters and finishers are judged on comparable shares of their work.

    Every eligible player

    Usual level against pressure level, both in run value per ball above what an average player would produce in the same situation. Players above the diagonal do better under pressure than they usually do. Dot size shows how many pressure balls each player has.

    How the rankings are built

    1. Model the chance of winning

      A logistic model with interaction terms predicts the batting side’s win chance before every ball, from runs, wickets, balls left, target, required rate, venue par and recent collapse. Out-of-fold Brier score 0.1755, against 0.1823 for gradient boosting.

    2. Measure leverage

      For each ball, leverage is the sum over outcomes of the outcome’s probability times the win-probability swing it would cause.

    3. Flag pressure

      The top 25% of leverage within each phase: powerplay, overs 7-10, overs 11-15 and the death.

    4. Score every ball against expectation

      Runs, dismissals, dots and extras are compared with what an average player would produce in the same state, against the same bowler type and batter hand. One wicket is worth runs.

    5. Anchor to the player’s usual level

      Pressure evidence is blended with the player’s own ordinary-ball level. The amount of blending is chosen to best predict held-out pressure performance.

    6. Weight and combine

      Components that repeat on held-out matches carry weight in proportion to how well they repeat. Rise versus usual level (15%) and consistency (10%) are fixed because the brief names both.

    How many of the model’s top 10 stay in the top 10 when one choice changes. Split-half reliability of the final rating, over 20 random splits of the matches: for batters, for bowlers.

    Limits

    • Batting ranks 6 to 10 are close calls. Each of those players makes the top 10 in only about a quarter to a third of resampled datasets.
    • Rising under pressure is a small, weakly repeatable effect. A player’s usual level explains most of his pressure performance.
    • The data has no line, length, field placings or usable ball speed. A tactical wide is inferred from the pattern of wides, not observed.
    • Leaving out the 2026 season keeps only five of the model’s top ten batters. Vaibhav Sooryavanshi has played only in 2025 and 2026.
